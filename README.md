# Where is our marketing money actually working? A funnel analysis in SQL

I built a small marketing database and used SQL to answer one question a small marketing team
has to answer constantly: **which channels actually produce paying customers, and which ones are
quietly burning budget?**

> The data is **synthetic** (generated by `generate_data.py`, seeded so it's reproducible). It models
> a B2B SaaS funnel: ~4,800 leads across 6 channels, 29 campaigns, 8 community events, and a
> 2-week landing page A/B test. The point is the analysis, not the numbers.

## Files
- `generate_data.py` builds `marketing.db` (SQLite)
- `analysis.sql` seven queries, each commented with the question it answers
- `marketing.db` the generated database (load it into Metabase via the SQLite driver to build dashboards)

## Run it
```
python generate_data.py
sqlite3 marketing.db < analysis.sql
```

## What the SQL found

**1. Volume is not value.** Paid Search + Paid Social bring in ~49% of leads but only ~12% of paying customers.
Community converts 29% of activated users to paid; Paid Social converts 6%.

**2. Unit economics flip the ranking.** Customer acquisition cost: Community ~$64, Newsletter ~$74,
Content/SEO ~$92 vs. Paid Search ~$1,158 and Paid Social ~$2,322. Paid Social returns about
$0.47 of first-year revenue per dollar spent; Community returns about $38.

**3. One campaign did real damage.** "Paid Social #5" spent $9,800 and generated 8 leads and 0 customers.
Query 3 is built to surface this kind of thing automatically, so it could become a standing alert.

**4. The audience hypothesis holds.** Analysts (9.5%) and engineers (7.4%) convert to paid at roughly
double the rate of marketing and product roles (~4.3%). That backs the idea of marketing to people who
look like the users.

**5. Events are efficient only when small.** Cost per attendee ranged from ~$13 to ~$108.
The cheap Austin meetups beat the expensive London one by ~8x on cost per attendee.

**6. The A/B test is a "keep running," not a "ship it."** Variant B lifted signups from 3.53% to 4.18%
(+18.5% relative), but the z-score is 1.92, just under the 1.96 needed for 95% confidence.
I wouldn't call it a winner yet; I'd extend the test rather than ship on a near-miss.

## What I'd do next
- Shift budget from Paid Social toward Community and Content/SEO, then measure whether those channels
  hold their conversion rates as they scale (they may not).
- Turn query 3 into a weekly Metabase alert.
- Track attribution beyond last-touch; this model credits one channel per lead.

## Limitations (being upfront)
Synthetic data, single-touch attribution, small event sample sizes, and a short A/B window.
Real results would need real data and a proper significance/power calculation before acting.
